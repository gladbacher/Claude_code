# Context — The Critique to Survive (Stevenson vs Neidle, Cynical Reading)

Imported from an external analysis (ChatGPT conversation, July 2026). A deliberately cynical reading of the Gary Stevenson / Dan Neidle disagreement — not endorsed as fact, kept because it is the strongest available critique of this series' own foundations, and because the failure modes it describes apply directly to this channel. The hostile pass draws on this file.

## The two cynical readings, condensed

**Stevenson (the model this series is built on):**
- "Telling people the rich cause all their problems is a scalable business model."
- Every issue becomes inequality; nuance loses views; each appearance needs a more apocalyptic prediction; trading success functions as appeal-to-authority outside his expertise; facts get *selected* for a commercially successful narrative — not lied about, selected.
- Philosophy: *approximately right about the big picture over exactly right about the details.*

**Neidle (the archetype of this series' most dangerous critic):**
- "Correcting other people's mistakes is an effective way to build authority."
- Attacks technical inaccuracies rather than directional claims, because errors are provable and values aren't; complexity flatters institutions; winning arguments over changing outcomes.
- Philosophy: *exactly right about the details, because otherwise the big picture can't be trusted.*

**The synthesis:** Gary is rewarded for making complicated problems feel simple; Dan is rewarded for making simple claims feel complicated. Both face audience capture — followers form expectations, and content drifts toward confirming them.

## What this series does with it

### 1. The Neidle attack is the threat model
The series will not be debunked by a billionaire — it will be debunked by a meticulous technician finding one wrong number and using it to discredit the directional argument. This is exactly why the quality gates exist (no number without a primary source; concede what's true first). Every episode should survive the question: *"What would a Dan Neidle thread about this episode say?"*

### 2. The hostile pass gets a second lens
The existing hostile pass attacks weak claims. Add the **technical pass**: check every number's definition and denominator (is "1.5% fees" platform+fund or fund only? is "a third of the pot" vs gross or net contributions? does "£15bn/yr" hold after behavioural response, per whom?). Where a number is contested, say so on camera — "estimates range from X to Y, and even the low end is a scandal" is *stronger*, not weaker, and it is Neidle-proof.

### 3. Audience-capture guardrails (the cynical-Gary reading, applied to ourselves)
- **No escalation ratchet.** Each episode's temperature is set by the evidence, not by the last episode's numbers. If the material is a 5/10, ship a 5/10.
- **No "everything is inequality."** One mechanism per episode is already the rule; this is why. If a story doesn't have a traceable money chain, it isn't an episode.
- **Predictions are ledger items.** Any forecast made on camera goes in the standing claims register (memory.md) and gets revisited on camera — right or wrong. Borrowed from the transfer series: the receipts cut both ways or they're worthless.
- **Concede-first is load-bearing.** The energy series concedes wholesale costs; the water series concedes legal overflows; this series should concede what the sceptics have right (e.g. where a specific wealth-tax design genuinely failed and why) before arguing the direction.

### 4. Content opportunity
The Stevenson/Neidle tension is itself an episode: **"The fact-checker and the trader — who should you believe?"** REBUTTAL-lens, steelman both, land on: precision and direction are not opponents; a movement that can't survive its fact-checkers doesn't deserve to win, and a fact-check that never engages the direction is a dodge. Positions this channel as the one that takes the strongest critics seriously — which is the moat Stevenson's cynical readers say he lacks.

## One-line rule for every script

> Directionally Gary, technically Dan. If the episode can't be both, it isn't ready.
